Independent Research · ibm_kyiv

Phase I: Bayesian-Augmented Zero-Noise Extrapolation (BZNE)

A Bayesian correction framework using classically simulated, calibration-informed priors to regularize noisy hardware measurements before zero-noise extrapolation, developed with mentorship from research scientist Dr. Malcolm Carroll at the IBM T.J. Watson Research Center. On a 3-qubit test circuit on ibm_kyiv, reduced mean absolute error by 87% relative to standard ZNE (0.040 vs. 0.306), improved extrapolation fit from R² = 0.962 to 0.9999, and reduced measurement variability by 91–95%.

Placed 3rd, Computer Science at the Regeneron Westchester Science & Engineering Fair (WESEF, 760+ competitors) and 1st, Computer Science at the Somers Science Research Fair (700+ competitors).

Code: github.com/samstryker/Bayesian-ZNE-Phase1

Independent Research · ibm_torino

Phase II: Shadow-Informed Priors for Bayesian ZNE

Extended the framework with priors derived from quantum shadow tomography rather than classical simulation, removing Phase I’s classical-simulation ceiling. Benchmarked on H₂ and LiH VQE circuits on IBM’s 133-qubit ibm_torino backend using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo posterior inference.

Shadow-derived priors did not outperform standard ZNE in raw accuracy under weak noise-scaling, but the framework delivered full Bayesian uncertainty quantification, hardware-specific noise characterization, and quantitative diagnostics of prior–likelihood mismatch. Submitted to the Regeneron Science Talent Search; technical paper available on request.
